# Project Vacuum — One-Page Pitch

**Hook:** You're a bug inside a handheld vacuum canister, and the human just hit the switch. Survive as long as you can while every pulse drags the whole ecosystem toward the filter. You can't attack. You can only dodge, and get your enemies to kill each other.

**Core loop (30–90 s runs):**
1. Dodge hunters and defenders in a big rounded-rectangle canister that fills your screen (WASD/arrows, Space = short dash with brief i-frames).
2. Read the pulse telegraph: a red rim flash, contracting rings, a countdown, and markers showing where new bugs and dust will land.
3. Ride out the pulse (the filter danger zone swells and pulls hard; bugs flee, but anything close or mid-range spirals into the core — lure them in) while using the chaos to get bugs to kill each other.
4. Use your one EMPTY pickup (in a corner): touch it to empty the canister, race to the flashing GREEN spot on the far side or be wiped with everything else, then a heavy refill drops in.
5. Die, see your time and best, hit **R**, and try again. Runs are short, so retries are instant.

**Bug types** (readable by color and silhouette):
| Bug | Role | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Roach (pastel peach) | Hunter | Always chases you. It's the baseline threat, a bit faster each pulse. |
| Wasp (pastel lemon) | Ranged shooter | Wanders until you get within ~300 px. Then it keeps its distance (150–235 px), strafes, and after a telegraphed windup (aim line and a glow, ~0.65 s) fires a burst of 1–3 stingers (more as the run goes on). They are moderate speed and dodgeable, and a stinger kills you but hurts any bug it hits first. It has 5 HP at full strength (2.25 early) and loses interest beyond ~400 px. |
| Spider (pastel lilac) | Slow weaver | Slow (46 px/s) and tanky (7 HP at full strength, 3.2 early). It lays web patches wherever it walks. **Webs never expire**: they sit there, fully visible, until something non-Spider crosses them (up to 200 webs; at the cap Spiders simply stop laying new ones and no web is ever evicted). Whoever crosses one is caught. You are slowed 50% on a single layer (a dash only 20%); one completed pass (stepping off, or 1 s standing) removes the whole web, whatever its layers. Bugs, Roaches included, are stuck at a crawl for ~1 s per pass, then the whole web is removed. Spiders are immune. Webs never kill. Close up (~85 px) a Spider does a telegraphed short lunge bite, which kills you and hurts bugs it hits. **Stacking:** if a Spider lays a web on top of an existing one it adds a layer (up to 4). Layers are drawn denser and brighter with an xN count, slow you more (50% on layer 1, up to 65% at 4), and keep bugs stuck longer, but since v0.17 a single completed pass by you or any non-Spider bug removes the whole web (only the webs actually crossed; the rest of a linked cluster stays). |
| Beetle (pastel mint) | Charger | Idles and wanders, and only reacts to YOU (within ~150 px). Then it winds up (orange ring, ~0.5 s) and charges in a straight line, hurting any bug in the way (2 dmg). It rests afterward. Squishy (4 HP at full strength, 1.8 early). |
| Moth (pastel sky; saturated cyan once recruited) | Ally (recruit it) | Drifts lazily in place, away from the filter zone, with a pulsing glow and a slow beacon ring so you can find it. Touch it to recruit it. Recruited Moths shield YOU: each one orbits you as an invulnerable escort and absorbs one hit on you. Stingers, charges, lunges, webs, bug fights and the filter all ignore it. The charge is spent only when something lethal actually hits the player: that Moth breaks with a shield-break effect, the attacker is stunned, and you get a brief grace window. An unrecruited Moth shields nobody. |

**Readability (v0.17).** You are the only saturated, glowing thing in the arena: a white bug with a cyan rim and glow on a dark contrast plate with an always-on halo, drawn above everything, with a dash trail, a landing ping and a locator arrow when crowded. Recruited Moths are saturated cyan too. Every other bug is a light, bright pastel with a crisp darker outline (still easy to see and tell apart by hue and shape); telegraphs (aim lines, red eyes, orange charge) stay bold.

**Score and run stats.** Survival time is still the main score, and best time is stored on its own. A provisional combined Score is shown on game over: time x10 + lured kills x50 + webs broken by you x5 + webs broken by bugs x2 + pulses survived x60 + canister empties survived x150. Best score is stored separately in localStorage (`vacuum_best_score`). Every run is also saved to `vacuum_runs` (last 25) as a record: survival time, pulses, empties, lured kills, webs broken (by you / by bugs), bugs killed, bugs lost to the filter, score and cause of death. 'Webs broken' counts each web removed once (v0.17). **Shared arcade leaderboard (v0.18):** an optional free Cloudflare Worker + KV (`leaderboard/`) keeps a Top 10 that friends can open from High Scores on the title screen and submit to from a clean High Scores panel that replaces the game-over screen; it is off until `LEADERBOARD_URL` in `game.js` is set to the deployed Worker URL.

Lethal contact: Roach, a lunging Spider, and a *charging* Beetle. Wasp stingers are lethal too. Webs are not (they only catch). Dust clumps are solid obstacles that block movement and stingers (a pulse scatters them rather than pulling them in). **Bugs don't hunt each other**: they aggro on you inside a per-type radius and de-aggro (leash) when you get far. Friendly fire is how lures work. A pulse does not eat webs and rarely eats dust (only a clump right next to the filter, ~60%): it gently SLIDES them. Every clump and every web cluster drifts 60–150 px over ~1.5–2 s with easing (no bouncing, no trails, little shake) to a new spot with a small dust puff, so the layout is mildly re-randomized each pulse. Webs laid next to each other (a Spider's trail) are linked: they are drawn with link strands and move together as one rigid unit, so chains and shapes survive. Nothing is destroyed or split: counts stay the same and webs keep their layers. Targets stay out of the filter danger zone and off the player's spawn point, and anything on the core is nudged out as a group. Webs only ever disappear when their layers are cleared by a pass from you or a non-Spider bug.

**Hp / damage ramp (v0.14).** Bugs spawn with 45% of their listed HP at t=0, rising to 100% at 90 s and on to 2.2x (about 1.8x at 5 min), and bug-vs-bug damage (stingers, charges, lunges) scales with ^0.85 of that. Early friendly fire therefore kills bugs quickly; late bugs are tough. Damage to the player and the Roach chase are unchanged.

**Arena (v0.14).** A rounded rectangle (~400,000 logical px², aspect clamped 0.5–2.6, scaled to the screen inside the safe areas below a slim HUD strip), with the filter at the centre. Bugs and the player clamp at the walls.

**Debris durability (v0.14).** Dust clumps wear down when bugs, you, stingers or charges hit them (cracks, shrinking), then pop in a small burst. A clump touching the filter core is destroyed, and a pulse can drag one that sits next to the filter into it (60%). Trickle spawns speed up below 8 clumps (cap 16). Webs are never destroyed.

**Vacuum pulse mechanic (v0.18 stronger):** The pulse is a recurring EVENT, like a storm circle. The first one hits at ~20 s, then the gaps grow slowly (20 s, 22 s, 24 s ... capped at 40 s). Each has a 3.5 s warning and a 3.6–6 s pulse; strength grows at half the old rate per pulse. The filter is a **danger zone**, not a vacuum that eats the arena.
- The filter has a lethal dark core and a visible orange danger zone around it. On a pulse the core and zone swell and pulse red/orange. Anything touching the core dies, and the player dies on core contact.
- Player pull: ~100 px/s growing toward 170 (below the 215 px/s run speed, +65% near the core within 160 px) — walk or dash out. Bug pull (v0.19): ~55 px/s growing to 120, up to +150% near the core (linear within 270 px). During a pulse EVERY bug (Roach too) flees radially at 1.2× its base pull, so net pull is inward inside ~234 px (slow at ~200 px, fast near the core: a death spiral) and outward beyond it (far bugs escape). Bonded Moths are exempt. Outside a pulse, non-Roach bugs still steer away from the zone. Dust and webs are not pulled: the pulse scatters them (dust limited by the 16-clump cap and durability).
- **Canister EMPTY (v0.19: player-triggered):** no timed empties. One EMPTY pickup per run sits in a random corner from the start; touching it triggers the empty and uses it up (locked during a pulse or its warning). 5.2 s telegraph with rumble and ONE flashing GREEN safe spot as far as practical on the opposite side from the player (≤ 860 px). Wipe kills almost everything outside the spot (and the player if they miss it); surviving = +150. Then a heavy refill of high-level bugs drops in — not a quiet reset.
- Between pulses / empties the arena escalates continuously: a bug every ~8 s early → ~1.8 s late, a dust clump every ~9 s → ~4 s. Caps 45 bugs / 16 clumps. Opening: 8 bugs (1 Roach) and 7 clumps. Trickle pauses during pulse, warning, empty and refill.
- Each pulse also drops a wave (5 + 1.5 per pulse bugs, up to 16, and 3–4 dust clumps) at the rim. New spawns get 4 s of filter protection. Roach chase AI is unchanged — lure him into other bugs or into the filter during a pulse.
- Lures work through friendly fire: stand so a Roach runs between you and a Beetle, or line up a Wasp's stingers through a Roach, or let a Spider lunge through something, or lure a Roach across a web so it gets stuck. Spawn mix is roughly even (Roach, Wasp, Spider, Beetle, Moth rare), so no type dominates.

**Controls:** desktop uses WASD/arrows + Space (dash), R restart, P pause. Phones and tablets are supported in Safari/Chrome: a floating virtual joystick appears wherever you touch the left half of the screen (analog speed), a DASH button with a cooldown ring sits bottom-right, and there's a small pause button. Tap anywhere to start or restart. Joystick and dash work at the same time (multi-touch). The canvas scales to the screen in portrait and landscape (the whole round arena is always visible, honoring iPhone safe areas), audio unlocks on the first touch, and scrolling, zoom and the pull-to-refresh bounce are disabled. Gameplay is identical on both. Keyboard input is hardened: it works from `e.code` or `e.key`, the canvas re-grabs focus on load and click, a real keypress on a hybrid laptop switches the touch UI off, and losing window focus pauses with a clear overlay that any key or click resumes. A **Share** button on the start and game-over screens shares the live link (native share sheet on iPhone, otherwise copies the link).

**Scoring:** Survival time in seconds is the score, with the best time stored in localStorage. Secondary stats on the game-over screen: pulses survived, bugs lured to death, bugs lost to the filter. These are good hooks for leaderboards or daily seeds later.

**Why it could sell (itch.io / Steam):**
- It reads in one sentence and a GIF: "bullet-hell survival inside a vacuum cleaner."
- The runs are short and the retry is instant, which suits itch.io jams, streamers, and "one more try" loops.
- The evade-only constraint plus the lure layer gives emergent stories ("the Beetle took out the whole Roach pack!").
- It's cheap to scale in content: each new bug is a behavior plus a pair rule. Possible additions are mutators, canister themes (dust bin, bagged, cyclone), daily challenges, and online best-time boards.
- The art can stay simple. The shapes and glow are already readable, so it can be done by a solo dev or a small team.

**Next steps:**
1. Playtest 5–10 people and tune opening density and pulse pacing against the 30–60 s first-run target. (The scripted dodge bot reaches ~35 s median, mostly dying to Roaches and fights, not the filter.)
2. Add 2–3 bug types (e.g. a Fly swarm, a Mantis ambusher, and a bug that eats dust) and an optional rare mutual-enemy flavour.
3. Add pickups or dust-based tools (such as dust clumps used as cover) and a stamina-based dash upgrade.
4. Add real art, SFX/music, juice, and gamepad support. (Touch controls are in; they need playtesting on a real iPhone.)
5. Add daily seeds and a leaderboard, then put up an itch.io web build and gather wishlist data before deciding on Steam.
